We work with architects, developers, investors and — since this year — other visualization studios, often at different stages of the same project. The 3D model and the craft are the same. What changes is what the output has to accomplish, and that decides how much of the work is rendering, how much is AI, and what comes first.

## For architects

A drawing communicates to other architects. It does not communicate to clients, planners or stakeholders who are not trained to read plans. Visualization bridges that gap — and, increasingly, it does so before the design is finished.

![An architect pinning render variations of a facade to the studio wall — the design conversation now happens over many candidates, not one image](/_img/articles/clients/architect.jpg)

We help architects:

- **Show clients what they are getting before they commit** — from a sketch or a flat CAD export if that is all that exists yet, with an AI image; from the model once it does.
- **Test options without re-rendering.** Two claddings, three colour schemes, morning and evening — material and design variants of an approved view are priced as variants, not as new images, because the pipeline generates them from the same base.
- **Identify design conflicts early** — spatial issues that are invisible in plan become obvious in 3D, and a correction at draft stage costs a fraction of a correction after finishing.
- **Win competitions** with clear visual communication on a competition timeline, where a basic render per view and a rendered set of elevations, sections and site plan are their own line items.
- **Explain complex details** — structure, circulation, phasing — to non-technical audiences, including a camera carried through four project phases as one package.

## For developers

Pre-selling units before construction begins is where visualization pays for itself most clearly. Planning permissions, investor presentations, sales offices, marketing campaigns — all of it runs on the same model.

![A sales office: the building on the wall, the floor plan on the table](/_img/articles/clients/hero.jpg)

We help developers:

- **Move units before groundbreaking** with photorealistic exteriors, night variants and photo montages into the real site.
- **Furnish every flat type without dressing every flat by hand.** AI interiors from the empty 3D model give each unit type a finished image for the sales sheet; a studio and a show flat get the full treatment.
- **Sell from a plan.** Unit floor plans, level plans and a sales-sheet template assembled per apartment — the "sales kit per unit" line on our price list — so the marketing team gets a complete, consistent package rather than a folder of renders.
- **Let buyers walk through it.** 360° tours generated from the same scene, a web 3D viewer with variant sets, and a presenter mode where the sales agent steers what the buyer sees on their own phone.
- **Feed the campaign.** A 60-second 3D film, social shorts cut from it, and AI motion from stills for the channels that need a loop every week.

## For investors

Before committing capital, you need to understand what you are actually funding — not just what it looks like, but how it sits in context, what the scale and quality of the spaces will be, how it compares to the brief.

![An aerial masterplan render in a boardroom — the picture is a decision-support tool before it is marketing](/_img/articles/clients/investor.jpg)

We help investors:

- **Understand spatial quality and scale before construction**, including aerial and site-plan views that put the scheme in its real surroundings.
- **Spot risks early** — design gaps, scale mismatches, context issues — while they are cheap to fix.
- **Compare scenarios.** Because variants are cheap, a board can see the scheme in two massing options or two quality tiers instead of arguing about one image.
- **Communicate the vision** to co-investors, boards and lenders in a form that needs no explanation.
- **Track design development** across phases from one model that is updated rather than rebuilt.
