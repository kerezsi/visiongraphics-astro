In early 2025 we published a version of this article that said generative tools "cannot produce images that meet the accuracy standards required for architectural visualization". At the time that was correct. We are rewriting it because it stopped being correct — not because AI became magic, but because we changed where it sits in the pipeline.

The short version: AI does not build the picture. It finishes it. Everything that makes a visualization *right* — the geometry, the camera, the proportions, the brand colours, where the designer put the furniture — still comes from a 3D model built from your drawings. Everything that makes it *read as a photograph* — material response, light falloff, reflections, atmosphere, the sense that a camera stood in the room — is now largely done by an AI pass that runs on top of that model, on our own machines, checked by an automated reviewer before a human ever picks.

## Where AI genuinely helps today

**Finishing.** This is the big one. The last stretch from "correct render" to "image that sells the project" used to eat most of the schedule: materials, vegetation, people, atmosphere. Our in-house system — [ArchUpgrade](/articles/from-render-to-photograph-archupgrade/) — takes the working render and produces photorealistic versions in about a minute per image per GPU, in controlled variations, each scored by a local vision model on five criteria before anyone looks at it. On a recent 18-view interior project the system generated over 300 candidates, the reviewer filtered them, and a human picked 18.

**Variants without re-rendering.** Season, time of day, weather, furnishing style, budget tier — from the same base render, geometry locked. Where we used to show you two polished options, we now show a curated selection from dozens. A night or dusk variant of an existing view is priced at a fraction of a new view on our [price list](/pricing/) precisely because of this.

**Relighting and sky work on finished images.** Time-of-day relighting, light migration from a reference photo, sky replacement and material swaps now run as targeted tools on a finished render rather than a full re-render. These are look passes, not physically measured ones — good for presentation, not for daylight studies.

**Interiors from an empty model.** For sales material, an empty 3D apartment can be furnished and finished by the pipeline from a viewpoint we set — a fraction of the cost of hand-dressing every flat type, and consistent across a whole building. The furniture is plausible, not specified; where a client sells a specific fit-out, we model it.

**Renders from sketches and flat exports.** A pencil sketch, a SketchUp or ArchiCAD export, an old render from a previous phase — all usable as a starting point for a photorealistic image before any 3D work begins. Fast for competitions and early client conversations; see [AI Render Upgrade](/vision-tech/ai-render-upgrade/).

**Motion from stills.** Camera moves, time-of-day and weather transitions generated from a still image, cut into 30-second to 5-minute films — [AI Animation](/vision-tech/ai-animation/). Right for social and sales-office loops; a complex flythrough through a building is still a 3D animation.

**Upscaling to print.** A 6-megapixel delivery upscaled to 4K or 8K with detail added rather than smeared, so an older render survives a hoarding-sized print.

**Everything around the picture.** AI narration and AI-composed music for films are now line items rather than a hunt for a stock library; batch quality checks catch a wrong-resolution frame before a client does; and the scripting that runs our 3ds Max pipeline is written with AI assistants — see [Automation, MaxScript and AI in 3ds Max](/articles/automation-maxscript-and-ai-in-3ds-max/).

## Where it still falls short

**New viewpoints.** An AI pass works from one image. It cannot walk around the building, look through a door it cannot see, or add the elevation you forgot to render. That is what the 3D model is for.

**Material-exact finals.** The pipeline generates surface quality probabilistically. It will produce a convincing oak floor; it will not reproduce a specific oak floor from a specific supplier's catalogue unless we model it. For material-precise output — a manufacturer's own product, a listed facade — a full V-Ray render from the model is still the answer.

**Geometry generation.** Text-to-3D and image-to-3D tools are in our toolkit for props and context, and they have improved a lot. They do not produce a building from a plan. The model is still built from the drawings, by someone who knows what a stair needs.

**Long, consistent video.** AI clips are 3 to 8 seconds each and hold a camera move about that long. A ninety-second film is an edit of many clips; a continuous walkthrough with exact geometry through every frame is a 3D animation.

**Judgement.** Which camera tells the story, which hour of the day the building looks like itself, whether an image will survive a planning committee — none of that is a parameter. Nobody has automated it, including us.

## What actually runs in our production

- **ArchUpgrade** — our own render-to-photograph system: multi-GPU ComfyUI farm, structured prompts, version tree, automated five-score review, delivery packaging. Daily use since spring 2026. The [technical write-up](/articles/inside-archupgrade-technical/) has the details.
- **Relighting, sky and material tools, generative expand, upscalers, camera-angle passes** — the same system, on finished images.
- **Local models only.** Every model we run is open-weight and runs on our hardware. No client image is uploaded anywhere. Each model is classified by licence, and anything non-commercial is locked out of paid deliverables at the configuration level — every delivered file carries a record of the tools that touched it.
- **A vetting database** of 1,537 open AI tools, re-checked weekly for release state, licence and hardware needs, so what we deploy is chosen on evidence — [how we vet the flood](/articles/how-we-vet-the-ai-tool-flood/).
- **Scheduled agents** that run generation and review unattended overnight, with a person reading the results in the morning — [the boring version of AI automation](/articles/agents-that-work-the-night-shift/).
- **Unreal Engine 5** for VR and interactive deliverables, with AI denoising and upscaling in the real-time path.
- **AI coding assistants** for every MAXScript and Python tool we build, plus an MCP server that lets an assistant drive 3ds Max directly.

## The honest position

We describe the change in one line: the job moved from *creator* to *curator*. The picture used to be made once, by hand, over days. Now it is made many times, by machine, in minutes — and the craft is in the model that anchors it, the constraints we put on the generator, and the choice of which result to show you.

It still does not replace 3D work. It is built on top of it. The accuracy comes from the model. The judgement is human. What changed is the iteration count and where the hours go.

For clients: when AI touches your project, we say so. An enhanced render from your approved model, an AI variant of an existing view, a concept image from a sketch — each is labelled as what it is, and each delivered file carries its provenance. You always know what you are getting.
